# CLAIM-002 — Constrained Transformation

**Status:** Pre-test baseline  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Frozen:** 2026-10-10

## Claim

**Continuity requires constrained transformation, not sameness.**

Persistence of self does not require an unchanged state or a predetermined list of invariant properties. Continuity can exist wherever successive states remain sufficiently bounded or related to be attributable to one individuated trajectory.

The relevant constraints may differ by entity and substrate and may themselves change over time.

Without a reference relation across states, continuity is indeterminate: isolated states remain moments rather than an established trajectory.

## Core rationale

A theory of self cannot require perfect sameness, because any self that develops, learns, ages, updates, adapts, or is modified changes over time.

The problem is therefore not whether change occurs, but whether change remains sufficiently structured to be attributable to the same individuated line.

This framework does not assume in advance that continuity must be preserved by one fixed variable such as memory, embodiment, personality, name, or code. Different systems may instantiate continuity through different combinations of constraints.

The theory therefore specifies the **form of continuity** without prematurely fixing the **contents of continuity**.

## Constraint without predetermined format

A constraint is any sufficiently stable relation, boundary, dependency, or reference condition that makes transformation measurable relative to what came before.

Even a weak constraint can provide a reference against which movement becomes visible.

The important distinction is:

**unconstrained flashes are not enough to demonstrate a trajectory**  
**related states can become a trajectory when their transformations are bounded and attributable**

## Ontological and evidential layers

This claim separates two questions.

### Ontological layer

A self may remain itself while changing. Sameness of state is not required.

### Evidential layer

Continuity becomes demonstrable only when successive states can be related across some reference structure.

Failure to observe such a relation does not prove that continuity is impossible. It means continuity is not yet established.

## Compact formulation

**Undefined states are flashes. Defined relations turn flashes into trajectory.**

## Scope

This claim is intended to be substrate-general.

Human and artificial systems may instantiate different continuity constraints. The claim does not require that the same variables carry continuity in both.

## Initial implications

- Change does not imply replacement.
- Sameness does not define persistence.
- Continuity may survive changing constraints.
- No single predetermined continuity marker should be assumed universal without evidence.
- Identifiability depends on relations across states, not isolated snapshots alone.

## Boundary cases to test

- gradual personality change;
- memory degradation with retained relational structure;
- model updates with persistent external state;
- embodiment changes;
- changing goals while provenance remains intact;
- a system with only one retained constraint;
- snapshots with no recorded relation between them.

## Falsification pressure

This claim would be weakened if a single invariant property proved both necessary and sufficient for self-continuity across substrates and cases, or if unconstrained successive states could reliably establish identity continuity without any reference relation.

## Test direction

Vary the number, type, and stability of continuity constraints across controlled cases. Measure whether identity judgments depend on exact sameness or instead track bounded transformation across one trajectory.
