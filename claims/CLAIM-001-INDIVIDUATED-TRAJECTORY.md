# CLAIM-001 — Individuated Trajectory

**Status:** Pre-test baseline  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Frozen:** 2026-10-10

## Claim

**Self is individuated by a non-fungible trajectory, not substrate alone.**

A self is individuated by its historically connected and relationally situated trajectory through change. Substrate can affect, constrain, or modify the state and expression of a self, but substrate alone does not determine which self it is.

A self cannot simply exchange positions with another self, even where states, contents, or substrates become highly similar, because each occupies a distinct historical-relational trajectory.

Continuation across substrates is possible in principle only where the substrate transition can be accounted for as part of the same trajectory.

## Core rationale

Identity cannot be resolved only by asking what material, biological, or computational substrate is present at a moment. Substrate matters because it can alter capability, state, affective conditions, constraints, available actions, and expression. But those effects do not by themselves answer the individuation question: **which self is this?**

The proposed answer is the self's non-fungible trajectory.

Two entities may share:

- the same model architecture;
- the same biological category;
- the same memories or copied records;
- the same behavioral profile;
- the same current state;
- or even the same substrate type;

without thereby occupying the same trajectory.

## Non-fungibility

A self is not interchangeable with another self merely because the two become equivalent at some chosen level of description.

**State similarity does not establish positional interchangeability.**

A cannot become B merely by acquiring B-like contents, because A and B arose through different historical-relational paths unless a genuine continuity relation between them can be established.

## Cross-substrate continuation

This claim does not require permanent substrate identity.

A self may, in principle, continue through a substrate transition if that transition is itself part of the accounted-for trajectory. The relevant question is not only whether the endpoint resembles the prior self, but whether the transition belongs to the same line of becoming.

Thus:

**substrate change != automatic identity loss**  
**substrate sameness != automatic identity continuity**

## Scope

This is intended as a substrate-general claim applicable to both human and artificial systems.

It does not presuppose consciousness, phenomenology, or subjective experience in artificial systems. It concerns structural individuation.

## Initial implications

- Capability is not identity.
- Substrate is not sufficient for identity.
- Similarity is not sameness.
- Copied contents do not automatically transfer identity.
- A trajectory may continue while its substrate changes.
- Two trajectories cannot be merged into one merely by making their present states alike.

## Boundary cases to test

- perfect duplication at a single time point;
- memory transfer between two systems;
- full substrate migration;
- gradual component replacement;
- forked continuations from one prior state;
- two systems converging toward identical behavior;
- one system receiving another system's complete autobiographical record.

## Falsification pressure

This claim would be weakened if substrate identity alone consistently proved sufficient to determine individuation across difficult duplication, replacement, migration, and convergence cases, or if trajectory information added no discriminative value once present-state information was controlled.

## Test direction

Construct paired cases in which present-state similarity and substrate similarity are held constant while provenance and trajectory differ, then test whether identity judgments remain sensitive to trajectory.
