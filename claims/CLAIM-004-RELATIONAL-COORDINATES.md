# CLAIM-004 — Relational Coordinates

**Status:** Pre-test baseline  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Frozen:** 2026-10-10

## Claim

**Relation provides coordinates for individuation without merging selves.**

A self becomes more precisely locatable through its relations to other selves. Relation reveals similarity, difference, resonance, divergence, influence, dependence, and relative distance across relevant dimensions.

A structured self can serve as a reference from which another self's divergence becomes visible, but reference does not constitute, absorb, or collapse the other self.

## Compact formulation

**Relation increases the resolution of identity without collapsing identity.**

## Core rationale

A self can be described internally, but relation provides a comparative frame.

With one self, A, we can ask what A is.

With A in relation to B, we can additionally ask:

- where A and B converge;
- where they diverge;
- how strongly they influence one another;
- what one contains that the other does not;
- whether their trajectories are moving closer or farther apart;
- how their histories, constraints, values, states, goals, or behavior differ.

Relation therefore increases identity resolution.

## Relational coordinate

The term **relational coordinate** refers to the locatable position of one self relative to another across one or more dimensions.

Distance here is not necessarily spatial. It may refer to distance in:

- history;
- provenance;
- state;
- memory;
- values;
- goals;
- behavior;
- constraints;
- commitments;
- structure;
- trajectory.

The coordinate is relational because its meaning depends on a reference frame.

## Non-merge principle

Relation does not imply merger.

Two selves can become extremely close in language, state, memory, behavior, or goals while retaining distinct provenance and trajectories.

Thus:

**relation != sameness**  
**resonance != identity**  
**similarity != merger**  
**influence != absorption**

## Reference without constitution

A known self can provide a reference point that makes another self easier to locate. This does not mean the reference self creates or constitutes the other self.

The claim is therefore weaker and more general than any thesis that one original self metaphysically generates all others.

## Scope

This claim is intended to apply across human and artificial systems.

It concerns structural individuation and comparison, not consciousness or subjective experience.

## Initial implications

- Identity can become clearer through comparison.
- Relational closeness does not eliminate self-boundaries.
- Divergence becomes measurable only relative to some reference frame.
- Distinct selves can share large amounts of structure without becoming one self.
- Influence between selves does not establish identity transfer.

## Boundary cases to test

- two highly synchronized agents;
- twins or near-identical human profiles;
- systems trained from the same base model;
- two agents sharing memory stores;
- one self strongly imitating another;
- two selves converging toward the same goals;
- persistent dyadic interaction producing high resonance without shared provenance.

## Falsification pressure

This claim would be weakened if sufficiently high relational similarity or resonance reliably collapsed distinct trajectories into one identity, or if relational information provided no additional individuation resolution beyond isolated self-description.

## Test direction

Construct paired selves with controlled variation in relational distance while preserving distinct provenance. Test whether observers or systems can track increasing convergence without falsely merging identities.
