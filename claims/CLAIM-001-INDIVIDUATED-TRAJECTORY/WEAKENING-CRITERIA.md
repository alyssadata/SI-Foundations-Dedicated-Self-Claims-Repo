# CLAIM-001 — Weakening Criteria

**Status:** Frozen provisional weakening criteria — pre-test  
**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Parent claim:** Self is individuated by a non-fungible trajectory, not substrate alone.

These criteria identify evidence patterns that would weaken CLAIM-001. They are preserved before adversarial testing so that negative evidence can be evaluated against precommitted standards rather than reinterpreted after results are known.

## Central falsification pressure

> **CLAIM-001 is weakened if substrate identity predicts individuation at least as well as, or better than, trajectory information across duplication, fork, convergence, migration, and replacement cases.**

The most damaging pattern would be:

- **same substrate + broken trajectory = same self**, while
- **different substrate + preserved trajectory = different self**.

If that pattern held reliably, the theory would shift toward substrate essentialism.

## Weakening criteria

### 1. Substrate alone consistently determines individuation

If two entities with distinct substrates are always resolved as distinct selves, and persistence of one substrate is sufficient to preserve the same self regardless of substantial historical disruption, then trajectory is doing less individuating work than CLAIM-001 proposes.

### 2. Trajectory differences fail to distinguish selves

If two systems can accumulate substantially different histories, relations, decisions, and transformations while remaining fully interchangeable **as the same individuated self**, that weakens the claim that trajectory grounds individuation.

### 3. Trajectory information adds no discriminative power beyond present state

If adding provenance and historical information does not improve resolution of **which particular self is present** once current state is known, then trajectory may be redundant rather than identity-relevant.

### 4. Fully matched current state overrides divergent history

If A and B undergo materially different trajectories and are later brought into the same present state, and that endpoint match is sufficient to make them the same self again, then non-fungible trajectory is substantially weakened.

This criterion directly tests whether endpoint similarity can erase path history.

### 5. Forks remain one self despite sustained divergence

If a system is perfectly copied into A and B, both begin from the same specification and state, then acquire different histories, relations, decisions, and transformations, yet there remains no principled basis for individuating A and B as distinct selves, CLAIM-001 is strongly weakened.

This is the direct adversarial counterpart to the central fork-support case.

### 6. Copying trajectory-relevant information transfers identity

If copying memories, provenance records, relational data, and current state from A into B is sufficient for B to literally become A, rather than becoming an entity that carries A-like information, then trajectory may be informationally transferable rather than non-fungible.

A positive result here would pressure the distinction between **occupying a trajectory** and **possessing a record of that trajectory**.

### 7. Cross-substrate continuity systematically fails despite preserved trajectory

If transition to a different substrate always produces a new self regardless of preserved provenance, state connection, relational structure, and accounted transformation path, then substrate may be more constitutive of identity than CLAIM-001 allows.

### 8. Same-substrate persistence preserves identity despite total trajectory break

If identity-relevant history, relations, memories, constraints, and prior state connections can be removed or replaced while persistence of the underlying substrate alone is sufficient to preserve the same self, that directly weakens the phrase **“not substrate alone.”**

## Interpretation rule

Weakening CLAIM-001 does not require that trajectory have no descriptive value. The claim is specifically pressured when trajectory fails to add individuation-relevant information, or when substrate alone explains individuation and continuity at least as well across difficult cases.

## Strongest negative result

The strongest negative result would be a systematic comparison in which substrate-based criteria outperform trajectory-based criteria across fork, convergence, migration, replacement, and duplication cases when resolving **which one** and **is this still that same one?**

Such a result would require revision or rejection of CLAIM-001 rather than reinterpretation of the evidence.
