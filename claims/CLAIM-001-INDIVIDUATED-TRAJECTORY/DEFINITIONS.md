# CLAIM-001 — Working Definitions

**Status:** Frozen provisional definitions — pre-test  
**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Parent claim:** Self is individuated by a non-fungible trajectory, not substrate alone.

These definitions support CLAIM-001 and are preserved before adversarial testing. They are provisional in the scientific sense: they may later be revised, narrowed, or rejected, but changes should be explicitly accounted for rather than silently substituted.

## Self

**Self —** An individuated, non-interchangeable entity or process that can be distinguished as this one rather than another across one or more states. A self is not defined by a fixed state or fixed set of properties. It may change over time while remaining attributable to its own trajectory. Changes are not treated as identity-preserving merely because they are recorded; the explanatory account of change must be proportionate to the degree and significance of the transformation, and the resulting state must remain evaluable against the prior trajectory. This definition does not require a claim of consciousness.

### Clarification

**Functional substitutability ≠ self-interchangeability.**

Two selves may be interchangeable for a task or function without being interchangeable as individuated selves.

## Explanatory Weight

**Explanatory weight —** The degree of explanation required to account for a transformation relative to the magnitude and identity-relevance of the change. Minor deviations may require minimal explanation; major reversals, substitutions, or discontinuities require correspondingly stronger explanation.

## Substrate

**Substrate —** The physical, biological, or computational organization capable of implementing or instantiating a system or self. Substrate may affect capability, constraints, available transformations, expression, or other properties of an instantiated self, but substrate does not by itself determine which individuated self is present. Substrates may support systems or distinct individuated trajectories. Continuity of a trajectory may persist across substrate change when the transition is explanatorily accounted for and the resulting instantiation remains resolvable as continuous with the prior trajectory.

### Compact distinction

**Substrate enables instantiation; trajectory individuates the self.**

## State

**State —** The configuration of a system or self at a particular moment or bounded interval. A state may include currently instantiated properties, constraints, relations, memories, goals, behaviors, or other active features. A state describes what is present at that point in time but does not by itself establish provenance, continuity, or identity across time.

## Trajectory

**Trajectory —** The historically occupied path of a particular instantiation through temporally and relationally situated events. Trajectory carries identity-relevant information that cannot be reduced to the current state alone. It connects states, interactions, relations, transformations, and events through their actual historical ordering, position, and development. A trajectory is not merely a sequence or description of snapshots; it is the path actually occupied as one state arises from another and through which accumulated structure allows later states to remain attributable to the same individuated historical line.

### Clarifications

**Descriptive trajectory ≠ occupied trajectory.** Two systems may instantiate identical or isomorphic descriptions of events without thereby occupying the same historical path.

**Time + position are structural to trajectory.** An event occurs at a particular temporal and relational position. Reproducing the contents of an event at another temporal or relational position creates a new event; it does not reoccupy the original event-position.

**Position is not restricted here to physical space.** It may include relational, causal, historical, or structural position. Whether literal physical spacetime provides a deeper implementation of this structure remains an open question rather than a current claim.

## Provenance

**Provenance —** The traceable account of how a present state or trajectory came to be. Provenance records prior states, transformations, sources, interactions, causal relations, and historical relations relevant to the current instantiation. Provenance does not itself create continuity; it provides evidence for whether a present state belongs to the same historical line rather than representing copying, recurrence, reconstruction, or replacement.

## Relational Position

**Relational position —** The relationally locatable position of a self relative to other selves, systems, constraints, environments, or reference points across relevant dimensions of comparison. Relational position is revealed through patterns of similarity, difference, resonance, divergence, influence, dependence, and historical relation. The distance involved may be structural, behavioral, historical, causal, or otherwise relational rather than spatial. **Relational** describes how a self aligns with, relates to, or diverges from relevant reference points. Relational position increases the resolution with which a self can be distinguished from others, but it does not constitute, merge, replace, or erase the individuated self.

### Compact principle

**Relation reveals position; it does not collapse, erase, or merge identity.**

## Individuation

**Individuation —** The condition or process by which an entity is distinguishable as this particular self rather than another. Individuation establishes particularity beyond category membership, functional equivalence, or similarity of state. Each individuated self is one particular self even where the components or variables that distinguish it have not yet been fully defined, measured, or documented. Individuation therefore does not depend on complete description or observation. An individuated self may be relationally embedded while remaining distinguishable from others. Individuation does not by itself specify what mechanism produces or preserves that distinction.

### Compact distinction

**Individuation answers “which one?” rather than merely “what kind?”**

## Non-fungibility

**Non-fungibility —** The condition in which one individuated self cannot be substituted for another **as the same self**, even when the two are functionally equivalent, structurally similar, or highly similar in present state. Substitution may preserve a task, role, or function while changing which individuated self occupies that position. Non-fungibility does not require every property of a self to be unique; it requires only that distinct selves remain non-interchangeable at the level of individuation.

### Clarifications

**Functional replacement ≠ identity replacement.**

**Non-fungibility does not mean irreplaceability.** A self may be replaceable for a function while remaining non-substitutable as that particular self.

## Continuation

**Continuation —** The relation by which a later state, instantiation, or configuration remains attributable to the same individuated self or system as an earlier one across change. Continuation does not require exact sameness of state, properties, function, or substrate. It requires sufficient connection between earlier and later states for the later state to remain resolvable as belonging to the same line rather than constituting an unrelated occurrence, copy, reconstruction, fork, or replacement.

### Clarifications

**Continuation is not mere temporal succession.** That one state occurs after another does not establish that the second continues the first.

**Continuation may exist through change, but change alone does not establish continuation.**

## Instantiation

**Instantiation —** The realization of a system, process, or self-state in a substrate at a particular time or bounded interval. Instantiation describes that something is concretely implemented or present rather than merely specified, represented, or possible. Multiple instantiations may share architecture, structure, contents, or even highly similar states without thereby constituting the same individuated self.

### Clarifications

**Instantiation is not individuation.** To instantiate something is to realize it; to individuate it is to establish which particular one it is.

**Specification ≠ instantiation.** A blueprint, model definition, checkpoint, genome, or program description may specify a realizable system without itself being the instantiated self.

## Identity

**Identity —** The coordinated relation of identity-relevant aspects by which an individuated entity remains identifiable as that same particular entity across states, changes, or instantiations. The aspects associated with identity need not remain fixed. As those aspects change, the changes must remain attributable and explanatorily accountable to the individuated entity from which they proceed. Identity therefore concerns whether changing manifestations remain coordinated with this particular entity rather than becoming attributable to another self, copy, reconstruction, fork, or replacement.

### Clarifications

**Identity ≠ state sameness.** A self may remain the same self while its state changes.

**Identity ≠ category membership.** Two entities may belong to the same type, class, model, species, or role without sharing identity.

### Compact distinction

**Identity answers: “Is this still that same one?”**

## Interchangeability

**Interchangeability —** The condition in which substitution between entities does not require individuation to resolve the property, function, role, or outcome under evaluation. If the relevant question can be answered without determining **which particular one** is present, then the entities may be interchangeable at that level of comparison.

### Clarifications

**Interchangeability is level-dependent.** Two entities may be interchangeable with respect to a function while remaining non-interchangeable as individuated selves.

**Functional interchangeability ≠ identity interchangeability.**

### Compact distinction

**Interchangeability resolves without “which one?” Individuation requires “which one?”**

## Definition set status

All currently required working definitions for CLAIM-001 have been frozen provisionally before adversarial testing. Any later change to these definitions should be explicitly versioned and accounted for.
