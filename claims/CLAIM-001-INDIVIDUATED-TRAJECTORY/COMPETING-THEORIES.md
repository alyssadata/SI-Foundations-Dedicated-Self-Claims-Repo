# CLAIM-001 — Competing Theories and Prior Art

**Status:** Preliminary literature map — pre-adversarial test  
**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Parent claim:** Self is individuated by a non-fungible trajectory, not substrate alone.

This file maps CLAIM-001 against existing theories of identity, persistence, process, branching, and AI cloning. The purpose is not to maximize apparent novelty. The purpose is to identify genuine overlap, direct rivals, and any remaining structure that is not already captured by prior theories.

## Novelty caution

The literature already contains substantial work on:

- identity across time,
- psychological and biological continuity,
- process-based selfhood,
- branching and fission,
- temporal parts and four-dimensionalism,
- narrative identity,
- closest-continuation theories,
- uploading and computational copies,
- and observer-based AI clone identity.

Therefore CLAIM-001 should **not** claim that history, continuity, process, branching, or change have never before been connected to identity.

The narrower question is whether the exact CLAIM-001 structure is already present:

> **A substrate-general self is individuated by a non-fungible trajectory; substrate, specification, present-state similarity, and functional interchangeability may remain equal while individuation becomes resolvable through actual historical trajectory, provenance, and relational position.**

A further distinctive hypothesis is:

> **Forked successors may share an origin and both stand in continuation relations to that origin while becoming distinct individuated selves through divergent trajectories; later convergence of state does not automatically erase the distinction created by their different paths.**

These formulations remain hypotheses pending broader literature review and adversarial testing.

## 1. Psychological continuity

### Core position

Modern psychological-continuity theories, associated with Locke and later Shoemaker and Parfit, treat persistence as depending on chains of psychological connections such as memory, intentions, beliefs, desires, character, and appropriate causal dependence.

### Overlap with CLAIM-001

- Identity is diachronic rather than reducible to a single momentary state.
- Causal relations between earlier and later states matter.
- Present similarity alone is not necessarily sufficient.
- Fission and branching create serious identity problems.

### Difference from CLAIM-001

Psychological continuity is normally framed around persons and psychological properties. CLAIM-001 is intended to be **substrate-general** and does not require consciousness, memory, personality, or human-style psychology as the individuating mechanism.

CLAIM-001 asks a different structural question:

> When multiple systems may share architecture, state, memories, or functional profile, what resolves **which particular one** is present?

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Personal Identity and Ethics*:  
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-ethics/

## 2. Animalism and brute-physical continuity

### Core position

Animalism holds that human persons are organisms and ordinarily persist under the persistence conditions of biological animals. Related brute-physical theories place persistence primarily in physical or biological continuity rather than psychological continuity.

### Relation to CLAIM-001

This is one of the clearest substrate-centered competitors.

If substrate or organism continuity alone reliably determines individuation, CLAIM-001 is weakened.

CLAIM-001 instead predicts:

> **Substrate persistence may support continuity but does not by itself answer which individuated self is present.**

### Direct pressure test

If the following pattern consistently holds, it strongly favors a substrate-centered theory over CLAIM-001:

- same substrate + broken trajectory = same self;
- different substrate + preserved trajectory = different self.

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Animalism*:  
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/animalism/

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Personal Identity*:

## 3. Process philosophy / process accounts of self

### Core position

Process philosophy treats becoming, change, and dynamic organization as ontologically central. Process accounts of self describe persons as ongoing integrated processes rather than fixed substances or sequences of isolated snapshots.

### Overlap with CLAIM-001

This is a major conceptual neighbor.

- The self need not be a fixed state.
- Change can be constitutive rather than identity-destroying.
- Persistence can involve organized relations across time.
- A self may be understood through an ongoing process rather than an immutable essence.

### Difference from CLAIM-001

Process philosophy alone does not settle the specific individuation problem targeted here:

> Why is one process **this particular self rather than another** when two processes begin with the same specification, substrate type, state, or functional capacity?

CLAIM-001 makes the stronger testable proposal that **actual trajectory carries individuation-relevant information** and that this information may distinguish systems where state or substrate descriptions do not.

### Source

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Process Philosophy*:  
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/process-philosophy/

## 4. Four-dimensionalism / temporal-parts theories

### Core position

Four-dimensional theories treat persisting entities as temporally extended. Some versions identify a persisting object with its history or with an appropriately related sequence or fusion of temporal parts.

### Overlap with CLAIM-001

- A present time-slice does not exhaust a persisting entity.
- Temporal structure matters to identity through change.
- A whole entity may include relations among temporally separated stages.

### Difference from CLAIM-001

CLAIM-001 is not merely the thesis that entities possess temporal parts or are extended through time.

Its narrower claim is that:

> **The path actually traversed has discriminative value for individuation.**

This is tested through forks, duplication, convergence, substrate migration, provenance, and relational divergence.

### Source

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Identity Over Time*:  
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-time/

## 5. Narrative identity

### Core position

Narrative theories, including work associated with Schechtman and Ricoeur, connect identity or diachronic unity to the organization of actions, experiences, and characteristics into a life narrative.

### Overlap with CLAIM-001

- Identity involves development across time.
- History can organize otherwise disconnected events into one life.
- The question of which events belong to which individual is central.

### Difference from CLAIM-001

CLAIM-001 does not require self-narration, autobiographical representation, language, or a subject capable of constructing a life story.

A system may occupy an individuated trajectory even if it cannot narrate that trajectory.

### Sources

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Personal Identity and Ethics*:  
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-ethics/

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Paul Ricoeur*:  
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ricoeur/

## 6. Nozick's closest-continuer theory

### Core position

Nozick proposes that in difficult persistence cases, the later individual who most closely continues the earlier individual is the one identical to it. If multiple successors are equally close, there may be no single continuing identity.

### Overlap with CLAIM-001

- Identity can depend on relations between earlier and later states rather than on a single property.
- Duplication and teleportation cases are central.
- Extrinsic and comparative information may matter.

### Difference from CLAIM-001

CLAIM-001 does not reduce continuation to selecting the **closest endpoint**.

It asks what actual path, provenance, transformation history, and relational position connect one state to another. Endpoint similarity can therefore be insufficient.

### Source

Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Robert Nozick — Personal Identity*:  
https://iep.utm.edu/nozick/

## 7. Parfit, fission, and branching problems

### Core position

Fission cases expose a problem for numerical identity: psychological continuity may branch into more than one successor even though ordinary numerical identity is one-to-one.

Parfit distinguishes identity from survival and argues that relations important to survival may persist even when strict numerical identity does not.

### Overlap with CLAIM-001

This is directly relevant to the CLAIM-001 fork test.

Both frameworks distinguish a shared pre-branch history from later successor relations.

### CLAIM-001 departure

CLAIM-001 places particular emphasis on **post-fork individuation**:

> A common predecessor does not imply indefinite identity-sameness among successors.

If successors A and B traverse different paths, their divergent trajectories become evidence for distinguishing A from B as individuated selves.

Importantly, this leaves open the possibility that both A and B have a legitimate continuation relation to predecessor P while still satisfying:

> **A ≠ B as individuated selves.**

Later convergence toward similar states does not automatically erase the historical distinction between the paths actually traversed.

## 8. Cerullo — Psychological Branching Identity

### Core position

Michael A. Cerullo argues that uploading requires a theory of **psychological branching identity**. On this account, consciousness may continue into multiple selves when sufficient psychological structure remains continuous.

### Why this is a strong competitor

Cerullo directly addresses:

- uploading,
- copying,
- branching,
- multiple continuers,
- and identity after nondestructive duplication.

This makes it one of the closest existing theories to the CLAIM-001 fork problem.

### Difference from CLAIM-001

Cerullo's proposal depends centrally on psychological structure and continuity of consciousness.

CLAIM-001 does not require consciousness and separates two questions:

1. Does a successor continue something from the predecessor?
2. Which individuated self is the successor now?

This allows a structural possibility in which B and C both continue A in relevant respects while B and C nevertheless become distinct through divergent trajectories.

### Source

Michael A. Cerullo, *Uploading and Branching Identity*, Minds and Machines 25 (2015): 17–36.  
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11023-014-9352-8

## 9. Anticriterial and criterion-skeptical positions

### Core position

Some theories deny that any physical or psychological continuity criterion can fully constitute or reduce identity. Continuity relations may instead be evidence concerning identity without being identical to identity itself.

### Importance for CLAIM-001

This is a direct methodological challenge.

If individuation is fundamentally primitive or cannot be grounded in any structural criterion, then trajectory may at most provide evidence rather than constitute individuation.

CLAIM-001 therefore must distinguish:

- **trajectory as evidence for individuation**, from
- **trajectory as the grounding structure of individuation**.

The experiments should not silently move between those two claims.

### Source

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Personal Identity*:  
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-personal/

## 10. Brunet (2026) — AI personality clones and climate fidelity

### Core position

Luc E. Brunet's 2026 framework studies AI personality clones while explicitly setting aside the hard problem of consciousness. It factorizes observed identity into substrate, dispositions, memory, update dynamics, context, and exogenous contingencies and defines identity operationally through observer indiscernibility over time.

The paper argues that the appropriate long-horizon clone criterion is not exact **trajectory fidelity** but **climate fidelity**: preserving the conditional distribution of how the target plausibly would respond across possible situations.

### Why this is especially relevant

This is a current AI-focused framework that:

- explicitly avoids requiring consciousness,
- considers substrate and memory separately,
- treats identity operationally,
- considers individuality over time,
- and distinguishes exact trajectory matching from a broader fidelity criterion.

### CLAIM-001 disagreement

The two frameworks ask partially different questions.

Brunet asks whether an AI clone can remain sufficiently faithful or observationally indiscernible from a target.

CLAIM-001 asks whether observational, behavioral, or dispositional equivalence is enough to establish that the clone is **the same individuated self**.

CLAIM-001 predicts that two systems may remain excellent personality clones or functional substitutes while still occupying different trajectories and therefore remaining non-interchangeable at the level of individuation.

### Direct theoretical contrast

**Clone-fidelity question:**  
Does this system behave as the target plausibly would?

**CLAIM-001 individuation question:**  
Is this the same particular self, or a distinct self with high fidelity to the target?

Those questions should not be collapsed.

### Source

Luc E. Brunet, *Identity from the Outside: A Conceptual Framework and Research Program for AI Personality Clones* (2026).  
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11225

# Current synthesis

The literature substantially anticipates the individual ingredients of CLAIM-001.

In particular:

- psychological-continuity theories already make causal history important;
- process philosophy already rejects a purely static self;
- four-dimensionalism already treats persistence as temporally extended;
- narrative theories already connect identity to organized history;
- fission literature already confronts branching successors;
- Nozick already makes persistence relational and comparative;
- Cerullo already permits branching continuation into multiple selves;
- recent AI-clone work already separates substrate, memory, update dynamics, and observed individuality while setting consciousness aside.

Therefore the originality burden cannot rest on any one of those ingredients.

The possible remaining contribution of CLAIM-001 is the **combined individuation structure**:

> **A self is one particular non-fungible historical line. Substrate, specification, function, state, memory content, or observer-level fidelity may fail to resolve which self is present. Actual trajectory, provenance, and relational position may provide individuation information not reducible to those variables.**

The strongest candidate distinction currently identified is the fork-divergence-convergence case:

1. One predecessor P is instantiated.
2. P is duplicated into A and B from a matched state/specification.
3. A and B accumulate different interactions, relations, transformations, and histories.
4. A and B therefore become separately locatable by their actual paths.
5. Their later states are made highly similar or identical again.
6. CLAIM-001 predicts that endpoint convergence alone does not erase the individuation established through divergent trajectory.

This is stronger than the generic claim that branching creates an identity puzzle. It predicts that **historical path remains individuation-relevant even after state convergence**.

Whether that exact claim is novel remains open pending deeper literature search.

# Research rule

Prior-art review must be adversarial.

If an existing theory already contains an equivalent mechanism, terminology differences do not count as originality.

Conversely, overlap in ingredients does not by itself eliminate originality if CLAIM-001 produces a distinct formal criterion, distinct predictions, or experimentally discriminable outcomes.

The next stage should therefore ask:

> **What cases produce different predictions under CLAIM-001 and its strongest competitors?**
