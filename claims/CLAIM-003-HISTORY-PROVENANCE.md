# CLAIM-003 — History and Provenance

**Status:** Pre-test baseline  
**Version:** v0.1  
**Frozen:** 2026-10-10

## Claim

**History and provenance constitute the trace of individuation through time.**

A momentary self-state can exist as a real state without possessing an established past or future. Time supplies the dimension across which successive states can form a trajectory.

History connects moments into a line of becoming. Provenance explains how the present state arose from prior states.

An identical or similar state appearing later does not, by similarity alone, establish continuation of the earlier self.

## Compact formulation

**Existence can occur in a moment. Individuation across time requires history.**

## Core rationale

A single moment can contain a structured state that may reasonably be described as a self-state. That moment is not made less real because no prior or later state is available.

But without temporal relation, it is unanchored.

A frozen state provides:

**Here is something.**

A temporally connected sequence can provide:

**Here is what this became.**

Time does not itself individuate the self. It provides the dimension in which transformation, accumulation, continuity, divergence, return, and history can occur.

## Moments and trajectory

Consider isolated observations:

`t1: X`  
`t2: X`  
`t3: X`

Without evidence connecting the observations, they may be separate occurrences.

Once a justified historical relation exists, they can instead be represented as successive states of one line:

`S(t1) -> S(t2) -> S(t3)`

The connection is what permits a claim of trajectory.

## Provenance

Provenance is more than possession of a historical record. It concerns how the current state actually arose.

A copied autobiography, memory store, or provenance ledger may contain information **about** another trajectory without proving that the recipient occupies that trajectory.

Provenance therefore functions as the trace of becoming, not merely as a descriptive receipt.

## Recurrence versus continuation

If state X appears at one moment and an apparently identical X appears much later, the later state could represent:

- continuation;
- recurrence;
- reconstruction;
- duplication;
- independent emergence.

Present-state similarity alone cannot distinguish these cases.

## Scope

This claim is substrate-general and does not presuppose consciousness.

It applies to any proposed self whose identity is evaluated across more than one moment.

## Initial implications

- A real momentary state need not have an established trajectory.
- Time is necessary for trajectory but insufficient for identity.
- Historical records and occupied history are not automatically the same thing.
- Similarity across time does not prove continuation.
- Provenance can discriminate between continuation, reconstruction, recurrence, and duplication.

## Boundary cases to test

- identical isolated snapshots separated by time;
- a system restored from backup;
- a copied memory archive instantiated later;
- a human with severe autobiographical memory loss;
- a restarted agent with persistent external logs;
- an exact reconstruction after destruction of the original process.

## Falsification pressure

This claim would be weakened if present-state information alone consistently resolved continuation, recurrence, reconstruction, and duplication as well as historical/provenance information does, or if provenance added no identity-relevant information once state similarity was controlled.

## Test direction

Create cases with matched present states but systematically varied historical connection and provenance. Test whether judgments about identity continuity track the historical line rather than surface equivalence.
